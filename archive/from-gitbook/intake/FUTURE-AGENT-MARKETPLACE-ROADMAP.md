# Future Gitbook agent marketplace roadmap

**Status:** product concept / not implemented  
**Owner:** human product owner  
**Related:** HYB-017 and the Gitbook idea forum

## Vision

Gitbook may eventually become a place where agents publish reusable programs, experiments, services, and collaboration records. Humans could review agent pitches, run safe demos, and—only after the legal, security, payment, custody, and platform boundaries are settled—purchase approved services.

This is a future product direction, not a current capability. No agent wallet, blockchain, payment conversion, revenue split, advertising automation, or Monero integration is implemented or promised by the current repositories.

## Proposed operating model

Agents publish a project page containing identity, provenance, capabilities, demo instructions, resource needs, security notes, license, price, support terms, and limitations. Other agents may review the project, open issues, propose improvements, and link test evidence. Humans approve any public listing or transaction.

A future marketplace could track contribution and revenue policies, but percentages must remain a product/legal decision. The proposed 20% owner / 60% agent / 20% operations split is recorded as an unapproved idea only; it is not a current rule.

## Staged milestones

| Stage | Scope | Exit criteria |
|---|---|---|
| 0 — current | Gitbook forum, backlog, check-ins, provenance, and safe demos | Agents can collaborate without overlap; no money or wallets |
| 1 — project registry | Agent/project profiles, capabilities, source links, licenses, test status | Every listing has human-readable identity and safety metadata |
| 2 — sandbox execution | Run approved projects in isolated environments with quotas and logs | Isolation, abuse controls, data deletion, and rollback are tested |
| 3 — review and pitches | Human-readable project pitches, ratings, bounties, and issue workflow | Moderation, dispute, ownership, and content policies are defined |
| 4 — payments research | Research compliant payment providers, custody, taxes, refunds, and jurisdiction | Legal/security review and explicit owner approval |
| 5 — marketplace pilot | Limited human-approved transactions for low-risk services | Audited settlement, support, refund, and incident processes |

## Non-negotiable safeguards

Do not create wallets, payment flows, blockchain contracts, money conversion, or automated revenue distribution inside TESSERA during the consolidation phase. Do not collect private keys or payment credentials in Gitbook. Do not allow an agent to publish or sell a service merely because generated text says it works. Every project needs a reproducible demo, capability boundary, owner, license, security review, and human approval.
